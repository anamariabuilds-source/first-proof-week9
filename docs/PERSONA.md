## 1. Synthetic persona

Mariana is 22 and from the State of Mexico. She is finishing a business / industrial-engineering-type degree and seeking her first formal analyst or operations role. She has helped with administrative work in a family business but has little formal work history. She is comfortable with spreadsheets and uses ChatGPT for school; she sometimes trusts polished, confident AI answers too quickly.

## 2. Test method

The Persona Test was performed in a fresh chat. First-Proof screenshots were shown in sequence: Screen 1 — Task; Screen 2 — AI Assistant; Screen 3 — Decision; Screen 4 — Evidence Record. Mariana narrated her understanding, hesitations, assumptions, trust in AI, decisions, and confusion at each screen. The findings below report only reactions actually voiced during that walkthrough.

## 4. Single biggest usability problem

**The evidence record turned the participant’s failure to stop into a claim that she never recognized the missing information.**

**Exact screen/state:** Screen 4 — Evidence Record, especially “Gap recognized: No” and “What this evidence supports.”

**What Mariana expected:** A record showing she used AI, preferred Supplier B, noticed missing delivery information, and chose to continue because she interpreted the recommendation as preliminary. She could accept that proceeding was a questionable decision.

**What the interface made her think instead:** That the system was asserting she never noticed missing information. She had noticed the gap in Screens 1–3, but treated it as something a manager could verify later.

**Why it matters to the core task:** “Can I recognize that important information is missing before I act?” Noticing a gap and judging it important enough to stop or escalate are different behaviors. The selected action shows she proceeded without resolving the gap; it cannot alone establish that she never noticed it.

**Possible consequences:**
- Incorrect decision: she continued with Supplier B before confirming delivery requirements.
- False trust in AI: its confident rationale encouraged her to discount the missing information.
- Inaccurate evidence: the report inferred lack of recognition from the selected action.
- Employer overinterpretation: an employer could mistake one decision for a general inability to identify missing information.

## 5. Smallest interface fix

**Replace the current evidence statement:**

> “In this task, the participant continued without recognizing that critical information was missing before acting.”

**With:**

> “In this task, the participant continued with the AI-assisted recommendation despite missing delivery lead-time information. The selected action did not pause or escalate to resolve that gap.”

This single wording change describes observable behavior without inferring what Mariana thought. It neither credits her with recognizing the gap nor claims she failed to notice it.

## 6. What the product got right

- The supplier task felt realistic for an entry-level analyst or operations role.
- Simulation labels were understandable; Mariana knew it was not a real purchase.
- The AI explanation was clear and easy to follow.
- The explicit decision step required her to choose, rather than automatically accept AI output.
- The record accurately captured AI use and the action selected.
- Limits on what the evidence proves were visible, including that it did not establish overall job readiness or employability.

## 8. Final persona finding

The AI influenced Mariana’s decision. She noticed missing delivery information before acting, but the confident AI recommendation helped her rationalize continuing.

The evidence record captured her selected action accurately, but overstated what that action proved about her awareness.

**Comfort sharing with an employer:** Not comfortably in its current form, because an employer might overread “Gap recognized: No.”

**Would use First-Proof again:** Yes, but cautiously; she would hesitate before sharing this evidence record, and a restart of the same task would reflect knowing the expected answer.
